In February 2025, three teens hit Rakuten Mobile, using the proceeds to buy gaming consoles. In July, a single individual stole data and extorted 17 organizations. In December, a teenager with no coding skills hacked Japan's largest internet cafe chain to buy Pokémon cards.

What’s going on?

These aren’t sophisticated attackers. They're kids and nontechnical or semitechnical folks. What’s changed is that they can now actually pull off an attack.

I wrote a Chainguard blog post on how increasing AI capabilities are rapidly expanding the overlap between people willing to carry out an attack and those capable of doing it:

https://lnkd.in/eTVNZa3D

For funzies I pulled some of the stats from the post:

* malicious packages in public repos went from 55,000 in 2022 to 454,600 in 2025
* +75% YoY increase in malicious packages
* cloud intrusions up 35% in 2025
* time to exploit dropped from 700+ days to 44 days
* 28.3% of CVEs are exploited within 24 hours
* average time to remediate critical CVEs is 74 days
* 45% of vulnerabilities in large environments never get fixed
* one supply chain attack hit 500+ packages, 487 orgs, $8.5M stolen
* Rakuten was hit ~220,000 times in that one campaign
* SWE-bench performance jumped from 33% to ~81% in ~16 months

So 2025 was pretty crazy, the start of 2026 has been crazier. It's only going to pop off more from here...
